● Getting glacier velocity from NISAR

  NISAR doesn't currently distribute a ready-made "ice velocity" data product
  off the shelf — per the Data Products Overview
  (https://nisar-docs.asf.alaska.edu/products-overview), the only Level‑3
  geophysical product listed today is Soil Moisture (SME2). Glacier/ice-sheet
  velocity is instead derived from the pixel-offset (speckle-tracking) products,
  which are explicitly built for cryosphere applications:

  1. Get the offset-tracking products from ASF DAAC
  - ROFF (https://nisar-docs.asf.alaska.edu/roff) — Level‑1, range-Doppler
    (radar) coordinates, 90 m ground posting. Dense pixel offsets from
    incoherent cross-correlation ("speckle tracking") between a coarsely
    coregistered pair of RSLCs.
  - GOFF (https://nisar-docs.asf.alaska.edu/goff) — Level‑2, the geocoded
    version of ROFF at 80 m posting, projected to UTM/polar stereographic —
    usually the more convenient one to start with since it's already
    map-projected.

  2. Derive velocity
  Each offset layer gives you range and azimuth pixel displacement between the
  reference/secondary RSLC pair. Convert those to ground displacement and divide
  by the temporal baseline (time separation) between the two acquisitions to
  get a velocity field. Layers are distributed raw — no low-pass filtering or
  outlier removal — so you'll need to do your own filtering/outlier rejection.

  3. Know the caveats (from Product Known Issues
  (https://nisar-docs.asf.alaska.edu/product-known-issues#roff-products)):
  - ROFF products over ice sheets currently have uncorrected ionospheric 
    distortion in the azimuth offsets (up to a few pixels).
  - The offset-tracking search radius is too small to capture fast ice motion
    (>~a few thousand m/yr) in some acquisitions — a known limitation planned to
    be expanded in future processing.

  4. Algorithm reference
  The authoritative algorithm document is the ATBD: "NISAR L3 Ice Sheets and 
  Glacier Products" (Joughin, Rignot, Forster, Gardner & Scheuchl, 2022, Rev
  2.1) — https://gitlab.com/nisar-science-algorithms/cryosphere/land-ice/atbd/do
  cumentation/-/blob/main/ATBD_IceSheetVelocity-Version2.1.pdf. This is where
  the actual velocity-derivation algorithm (built on top of ROFF/GOFF offsets)
  is specified, ahead of it becoming a distributed L3 product.
