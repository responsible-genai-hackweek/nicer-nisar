● Here's how NISAR supports glacier/ice velocity estimation, based on the NISAR
  Data User Guide (nisar-docs.asf.alaska.edu):

  Primary products — pixel offset tracking
  - GOFF (Geocoded Pixel Offsets, Level 2) and ROFF (Range-Doppler Pixel
    Offsets, Level 1) are the products explicitly built for cryosphere work.
    They're generated by speckle/offset tracking between a pair of coarsely
    coregistered RSLC (Single Look Complex) acquisitions.
    - Contents: range and azimuth pixel-offset layers + SNR layers, at 80–90 m
      posting.
    - These are intentionally raw/unfiltered — the docs note they "may contain
      offset outliers and are not low pass filtered," so you're expected to do
      your own outlier removal/filtering.
    - GOFF is geocoded (UTM or Polar Stereographic); ROFF stays in native radar
      (range-Doppler) coordinates.
    - Products overview (https://nisar-docs.asf.alaska.edu/products-overview) →
      GOFF (https://nisar-docs.asf.alaska.edu/goff) / ROFF
      (https://nisar-docs.asf.alaska.edu/roff)

  Secondary route — InSAR phase
  - RUNW/GUNW (unwrapped interferograms) are primarily aimed at solid-earth
    deformation but can inform slower ice dynamics where coherence is retained.
    Offset tracking (GOFF/ROFF) is the go-to for fast-moving glacier/ice-sheet
    flow, since large motion decorrelates phase and exceeds unwrapping limits.

  What the user guide doesn't spell out (I checked and couldn't confirm this
  from the docs, so flagging rather than guessing):
  - The exact conversion from pixel offsets → ground displacement → velocity
    (needs incidence-angle geometry and the temporal baseline between the
    acquisition pair) isn't detailed in the overview pages — that level of
    detail lives in the Product Specification Documents and Algorithm
    Theoretical Basis Documents (ATBDs) linked from the main guide.
  - No NISAR-specific tooling (e.g., autoRIFT/ITS_LIVE-style velocity pipelines)
    is mentioned in the Tools & Services section — HyP3 currently only lists
    planned support for GUNW and Level 3 products, not offset-tracking
    workflows.

  Where to go next

    (https://nisar-docs.asf.alaska.edu/products-overview) and Applications
    pages)
  - Python tutorials (https://nisar-docs.asf.alaska.edu/python-tutorials#nisar-s
    cience-algorithms) — may include worked algorithm notebooks
  - Earthdata Forum
    (https://forum.earthdata.nasa.gov/viewforum.php?f=7&keywords=NISAR) or
    uso@asf.alaska.edu for direct questions on velocity-derivation methodology
